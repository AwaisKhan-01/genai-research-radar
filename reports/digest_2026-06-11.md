### FACTR 2: Learning External Force Sensing for Commodity Robot Arms Improves Policy Learning
[Read Full Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12406v1)

**The TL;DR:** This paper introduces a data-driven method (NEXT) to estimate external forces on commodity robot arms without dedicated sensors, and a training technique (FIRST) that leverages these estimates to significantly improve policy learning for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

**Architectural Innovation:**
The core innovation is **Neural External Torque Estimation (NEXT)**, a novel neural network architecture trained on self-motion data to infer external joint torques, effectively acting as a high-fidelity virtual force sensor. This model provides crucial perceptual input to downstream policy learning systems. Additionally, **Force-Informed Re-Sampling Training (FIRST)** is an innovative data sampling strategy applied during behavior cloning, which intelligently up-samples critical pre-contact and contact phases to enhance policy robustness rather than modifying the policy network architecture itself.

**Engineering Takeaway:**
This research provides tangible value by enabling advanced force-aware capabilities on low-cost, off-the-shelf robots, significantly reducing hardware expenditure for production Generative AI robotic deployments. The **NEXT** method can provide critical virtual sensor data (real-time force estimates) to GenAI models that generate robot actions or policies, making them more aware of their physical environment without requiring expensive physical sensors. The **FIRST** training strategy can be directly integrated into existing behavior cloning or policy learning pipelines for robot control, substantially improving the robustness and success rate of generated robot behaviors (over 17% gain reported) for contact-rich tasks. This translates to faster development cycles, more reliable real-world applications, and the ability to expand GenAI applications into more complex, interactive physical tasks previously limited by hardware cost or sensing capabilities. The rapid training time (1 minute from 10 minutes of data) for NEXT further implies quick calibration and deployment for diverse robot platforms.

### World Pilot: Steering Vision-Language-Action Models with World-Action Priors
[Read Full Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12403v1)

**The TL;DR:** World Pilot significantly improves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by integrating dynamic scene evolution and anticipated action trajectory priors from a separate World-Action Model, leading to state-of-the-art performance and robustness.

**Architectural Innovation:** The core innovation involves augmenting an existing VLA policy with two distinct priors from a World-Action Model (WAM), routed into its decision chain:
1.  **Latent Steering:** Conditions the VLA's *perception layer* with a *scene-evolution latent* from the WAM, providing an anticipated view of the scene's future state.
2.  **Action Steering:** Supplies an *anticipated trajectory* as a *motion prior* to the VLA's *action generator*, providing a trajectory-level hint for continuous actions.
This framework effectively injects dynamic, temporal understanding into VLAs primarily trained on static data, making them more suitable for continuous, contact-rich processes.

**Engineering Takeaway:** This approach offers a tangible pathway to enhance the performance and reliability of production Generative AI pipelines in robotic control and dynamic interaction tasks:
*   **Improved Real-World Robustness:** By incorporating dynamic priors, GenAI agents can achieve higher success rates and greater resilience to real-world shifts in viewpoint, geometry, and object deformation, which is critical for reliable deployment in industrial or service robotics.
*   **Enhanced Manipulation Capabilities:** For products requiring fine motor control or contact-rich interactions (e.g., assembly lines, dexterous manipulation), this method significantly boosts an agent's ability to handle the continuous, dynamic nature of such tasks.
*   **Cost-Effective Augmentation:** The finding that scene-evolution priors can be effective even from video-pretrained world models (without action-post-training) suggests a potentially lower data and training cost for building the WAM component, offering a more efficient way to upgrade existing VLA systems without a full architectural overhaul or retraining from scratch.
*   **Better Anticipatory Control:** Providing the VLA with an "anticipated view" and "motion hint" allows for more proactive and intelligent control, reducing reactive errors and improving overall task efficiency and safety in real-world GenAI applications.
